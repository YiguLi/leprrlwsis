## 2. Synthesis

### A. Is publishing such a configuration table common practice?
**No single convention; it splits into three tiers.**
- **Tier 1 — classic wake-steering / field-campaign papers** (Fleming 2017, 2019, 2020; King 2021; Howland 2019, 2022): report the *physics choices by citation* (e.g. "primarily based on the Jensen model" + "the Jimenez model"; "wake recovery and redirection models of Bastankhah & Porte-Agel and Niayifar & Porte-Agel") plus layout/spacing, one or two wind speeds, TI, and yaw bounds. They essentially never give a version string, wake switches, combiners, density, turbulence model or actuator dynamics. Of the four Tier-1 papers I read fully, exactly one names a FLORIS version (Fleming 2019: "NREL: FLORIS, Version 1.0.0").
- **Tier 2 — model-comparison / validation papers**: the most reproducible. Siguenza-Alvarado et al. 2026 gives a per-case table (TI, shear exponent, veer slope, Monin-Obukhov L, U_h, theta_h) and compares four explicit deficit/turbulence/superposition/deflection combinations (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21035v2). Dittmer et al. 2024 names both wake models, all three software environments and where each is used (https://elib.dlr.de/211413/). King et al. 2021 states its case matrix inline (§3-§4: 8 m/s, 6 %/10 % TI, 7D and 6D rows, 20/25 deg yaw).
- **Tier 3 — benchmark / environment papers**: most complete. WFCRL has a dedicated env-characteristics appendix plus explicit layout, action/observation and steady-state statements (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13592, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ifpen/wfcrl-env/main/README.md). WindGym documents configuration through YAML files rather than a paper table (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DtuWindEnergy/windgym/main/README.md).

Typical detail level: 6-12 of the 17 fields, and the *same* 5-6 fields are missing in almost every paper.

### B. De-facto standard / modal values
| Field | Modal value / range |
|---|---|
| FLORIS version | **not reported** (modal). Only explicit value seen: **v1.0.0** (Fleming 2019) |
| Velocity model | **Gaussian / Bastankhah** in modern work (King 2021; Fleming 2019 citation chain; Dittmer 2024). Jensen multi-zone in pre-2018 FLORIS (Fleming 2017) |
| Deflection | **Jimenez** in old FLORIS (Fleming 2017); Gaussian + secondary-steering/GCH in modern (King 2021) |
| Turbulence model | **not reported** (never named in any paper I read) |
| Combination model | **not reported** (never named; FLORIS default implied) |
| enable_secondary_steering / yaw_added_recovery / transverse_velocities | **not reported** in every paper read (pre-v3 papers predate the flags; King 2021 introduces the physics without flag names) |
| TI | **6 % and 10 %** as the simulation pair (King 2021); field range 5-10 % (Lillgrund 5.18-8.81 %; Howland 5 +/- 2.5 %) |
| Air density | **not reported** — no numeric value in any paper read |
| Turbine | **NREL 5MW** for simulation studies (WFCRL explicit: "used by the majority of proposed evaluations of RL methods"); real turbines in field work: Envision EN136/4 MW, Siemens SWT-2.3-93, ~2 MW / ~120 m rotor (India) |
| Power/thrust curves | **real tables**, not analytic: FAST-derived (Fleming 2017), FLORIS-internal Cp table (Fleming 2019), Bladed (Lillgrund), NREL published (WFCRL) |
| D, z_h | reported only in field/model-validation work: 136 m (EN136); 93 m / 65 m (Lillgrund); ~120 m / ~100 m (India) |
| Layout | **single streamwise row of 3-5 turbines at 6-8D** for model development; named farms Horns Rev 1 (76) / Horns Rev 2 (92) / Ormonde (31) / Westermost Rough (36) / Lillgrund (48) for benchmarks |
| Wind speed | **8 m/s** is the modal single simulation condition (King 2021; Fleming 2017 SOWFA); 6-8 m/s for validation; Region II = 4-12 m/s |
| Wind direction | either a single aligned direction or 5 deg sweeps around alignment |
| Yaw bounds | **20 deg** (Fleming 2019, load-driven) and **25 deg** (Fleming 2017 hard safety limit; King 2021 five-turbine; Howland 2022 +/-25 deg) are modal; +/-30 deg appears only as a yaw-sweep range for curve generation (Lillgrund) |
| Yaw rate limit | **not modelled / not reported** (modal) |
| Update / decision interval | 1 h on-off toggling (Fleming 2017, 2019); 2.5 h switching (Howland 2022); 10-min SCADA windows (Lillgrund); per-episode-step in RL envs |
| Steady-state assumption stated | **minority** state it explicitly (Fleming 2017 "in steady state"; WFCRL; Lillgrund "steady-state analytical wake models"); most leave it implicit |

### C. Fields papers MOST OFTEN OMIT (ranked)
1. **FLORIS version** — the single most common gap (only 1 of 4 Tier-1 papers gives one).
2. **Turbulence model** — never named in any paper read; King 2021 even notes its model needs "a more rigorous turbulence model" without saying which one it used.
3. **Combination/superposition model** — never named; only *studied* in a dedicated MDPI paper (title only; not read).
4. **The `enable_*` wake switches** — never reported (largely because they postdate most papers).
5. **Air density** — never given numerically.
6. **Yaw rate limit / actuator dynamics** — acknowledged qualitatively at best (Fleming 2017: "within the limits of yaw control actuation").
7. **Control update interval as a design number** — replaced by field-campaign toggling windows.
8. **Explicit steady-state / quasi-steady statement** — implicit in most.
Also often omitted: rotor diameter and hub height (simulation papers, because the turbine is assumed), and the provenance of the Cp/Ct curves.

### D. Precedent for an IDEALISED turbine with constant Cp = 4a(1-a)^2 at a = 1/3?
**Honest verdict: I found essentially NO precedent in the FLORIS / wake-steering literature.**
What the searches did surface:
- General Betz-limit exposition in theses/teaching material (e.g. https://bora.uib.no/bora-xmlui/bitstream/handle/11250/3071449/HTEK399_Masters_projects_TE.pdf) — not a wind-farm-control configuration.
- FLORIS's own turbine machinery (`power_thrust_table`, `controller_dependent_operation_model.py`, https://github.com/NatLabRockies/floris/blob/main/floris/core/turbine/controller_dependent_operation_model.py) and a GitHub discussion "Lack of Thrust in new turbine creation" — i.e. an analytic custom turbine is *implementable* in FLORIS, but I found no peer-reviewed paper using a Betz-optimal constant-Cp turbine.
- The adjacent conventions that DO exist: (i) **cos^p(gamma) yaw-loss power models** — Fleming 2017 fits pP = 1.41; Howland 2022 explicitly tests the assumption P(gamma) ~ cos^3(gamma) — but these multiply a *real* power curve, they do not replace Cp; (ii) fixed-induction **actuator-disc/actuator-line idealisations in LES** where a is held constant (Betz-optimal or otherwise) — standard in CFD-based farm studies, not in FLORIS; (iii) analytic/idealised turbines in purely analytical farm models (constant Ct, farm-averaged Cp), e.g. the JFM "two-scale interaction of wake and blockage" line of work.
So: **no citable precedent found for a constant Cp = 4a(1-a)^2 with a fixed at 1/3 inside FLORIS.** Markov this as a genuine gap — defensible as novel, but nothing to cite as authority. (Search coverage caveat: PDF-only sources and paywalled pages could not be searched systematically.)

### E. Papers that explicitly model yaw rate limits, deadbands or wake transport delay with FLORIS
- **Wang, Dong & Zhao 2026**, J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 3224:032066 — the clearest precedent. Multi-agent PPO wake steering run **"using the FLORIS wake model with realistic actuator rate constraints"**, motivated by "the slow dynamics and physical rate saturation of yaw actuators"; compares a heuristic Partial Time-Shift, a state-informed RL policy, and a QP integration; QP reaches 13.00 % power gain vs 12.00 % for the rate-unaware controller and reduces actuator duty cycle. Numeric rate limit is in the PDF body (not read). Abstract: https://zenodo.org/records/20715588
- **WFCRL** — states the boundary explicitly: on FLORIS "theta_i is always equal to the current value of the actuators" (no actuator dynamics), whereas FAST.Farm "adds an inner control loop at the level of the actuators [which] adds a response delay"; rate limits are imposed only through bounded *increments* in the action space. https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13592
- **Wake transport delay** is handled by *switching simulator*, not by FLORIS: WFCRL uses FAST.Farm because "under dynamic conditions there is a significant delay between the time agents take an action and the time this action finally impacts the turbines downstream". A separate dynamic-wake-model line exists ("Development of a dynamic wake model accounting for wake advection delays and mesoscale wind transients", J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 2265:022055, 2022) — found, NOT READ.
- **Yaw deadband**: Hulsman et al. 2026, J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 3224:032115 explicitly tests a yaw deadband theta_WD in [-5 deg, 5 deg] — but in a **wind tunnel**, not FLORIS. https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/3224/3/032115
- **Yaw hysteresis / resolution**: Howland et al. 2022 widens its analysis window to "account for hysteresis in the yaw controller" and notes 5 deg typical yaw resolution — custom model, not FLORIS. https://arxiv.org/html/2202.06683
- **Fleming 2017** acknowledges offset tracking "must be done within the limits of yaw control actuation" but reports no number.
- Conclusion: **no peer-reviewed FLORIS paper I found models yaw rate limits natively inside FLORIS.** The rate/deadband/delay constraint is always added in the controller, optimiser or RL layer, or by moving to a dynamic simulator. (FLORIS's own documentation/issue tracker was not systematically verified for native support — flagged as unverified.)
